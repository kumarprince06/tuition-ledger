# ADR-0017 Recorded class library as a paid add-on billed through the fee engine
**Context.** Some centres record classes and want students to revise from them, and want to charge for it. Video hosting is expensive and operationally different from everything else in the product; live classes are explicitly out of scope.
**Decision.** Recordings are an add-on (phase 4): uploads go to a video provider (Bunny Stream or Mux; chosen by cost per GB streamed at the time) that transcodes and streams; our VPS never serves video. Access is sold by the centre as a monthly per-student charge created by the existing fee engine, so dues, reminders, receipts and the ledger need no new money code. A student portal with OTP login shows entitled recordings; playback uses short-lived signed URLs with a name watermark and no download. Tuition Ledger charges the centre per active viewer plus storage, metered monthly like students and messages.
**Consequences.** The add-on reuses the fee, ledger and messaging guarantees; the only new infrastructure is the provider integration and the portal. Cost is visible to the centre before enabling. No DRM: the watermark and short-lived URLs deter casual sharing; determined piracy is accepted as a limitation. Entitlement checks are the one new security surface and are tested like tenant isolation.
**Alternatives.** Self-hosted video: bandwidth cost and ops out of proportion. Free recordings for all students: no revenue to cover the provider cost; centres asked to charge. Live classes: a different product.
